PROTAGONIST — [NAME TBD]
Age: Mid-forties
Occupation: Mid-level professional. Competent. Unremarkable by choice.
[v2] Specifically: senior claims analyst at Halbard Mutual, a regional
insurer. Fourth floor. Eleven years in post. Prices other people's
catastrophes for a living.
Core Wound: A decision made fifteen years ago that they have never 
spoken about. They built their entire life as architecture around this 
silence.
The Lie: "I chose this life." (The truth: they fled into it.)
Arc: From maintained stillness to forced movement. From watching to 
being seen. From the window to the door.
Voice: Precise. Observant. Slightly detached. Uses humour as deflection. 
Notices details others miss but refuses to act on them.
Physical detail: Always slightly cold. Wears an extra layer even in 
summer. Hands are dry. They notice this about themselves.
[v2] Private theory — The Conservation of Stillness: a household
contains a fixed quantity of event. If one keeps perfectly still,
the events go and happen to someone else. They half-believe it.
They would deny it under oath. It is the Lie's intellectual
scaffolding, and they describe it, when drunk, as "basically actuarial."
[v2] The humour is dry, exact, slightly too quick. It works on strangers.
It does not work on anyone who has been in their kitchen.

SARAH — The Colleague
Age: Early thirties
Role: The external pressure. The question the protagonist can't avoid.
Function: Sarah sees the protagonist clearly. This terrifies the 
protagonist because being seen means being accountable.
Voice: Direct. Warm but not soft. Asks questions that sound casual but 
aren't.
Key line (Chapter 5): "You don't have to keep pretending this is what 
you wanted. I'm not asking you to fix it. I'm asking you to admit it."
[v2] Kitchen access: In Chapter 3 Sarah's car fails its MOT. She lives
four streets away. The protagonist offers a lift for "a week or two" —
the offer is made before they can stop it. From Chapter 4 (Tuesday) Sarah
arrives at 7:40, lets herself in by the back door, and makes her own tea.
The office relationship moves into the house. That is the point.
[v2] Sarah keeps her tea in the middle dresser drawer. She put it
there herself on day one (Ch 4). She does not know the drawer locks.
[v2] Sarah's car is repaired at the end of Chapter 5. From Chapter 6
she no longer comes to the kitchen. Her absence is felt.

THE FIGURE IN THE WINDOW — [NAME TBD / MAY REMAIN UNNAMED]
Role: Mirror. The protagonist's future self if they don't move.
Function: Appears in Chapters 1, 7, 19, and 24. Each appearance 
recontextualises the previous one.
Key rule: The figure never speaks. Their meaning is entirely projected 
by the protagonist. The reader must be able to interpret the figure 
differently each time.
