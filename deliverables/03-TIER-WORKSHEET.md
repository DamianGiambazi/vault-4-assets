# The Tier Worksheet — a lens, not a score

Use it to see where a prompt (or a whole AI workflow) stands, and what the next step up would take. It describes; it doesn't measure. The measurement is your sealed key.

| Tier | Meaning (general) | A prompt at this tier | What lifts it to the next |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F** | broken, counterproductive | no task, or a task that contradicts itself | say what you want |
| **D** | works only in narrow cases | a task with no context: "make it better" | name the task and the audience |
| **C** | functional but flawed | a clear task, no context or limits: *"Write the scene beats for Chapter 4."* | ask the receiver what it needs (the 100% question) |
| **B** | competent; gets the job done | context supplied, nothing enforced | put the rules in `<constraints>` and ask for a checkable `<output_format>` |
| **A** | high-performing, reliable | the five fields filled, the output checked outside the model | seal a key before running; separate the writer from the critic |
| **S** | best practice | contracts, independent checks and the human decision together, with a record of what crossed | — |

## Vault 4 at each step
C: the cold-open draft · B: after C, the rules are known · A: Contracts 1–5, each checked by `v4_check.py` · S: all six rubric lines hold, the paste log is clean, and the author commits.
