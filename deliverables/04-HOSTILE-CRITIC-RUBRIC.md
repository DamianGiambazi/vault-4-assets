# The Hostile Critic rubric — what counts as debt

Contract 3 makes the same model audit the work it (or another chat) produced. It points at debt and fixes nothing. This sheet says what to look for in its report, and how to judge it.

## Run it well
- **A fresh chat**, not the one that wrote the work: the critic must not remember wanting to please.
- **Give it the rules, not the answer key.** It gets the SSOT and the manifest; never the key.
- **"No band-aids. Point at debt only. Do not fix."** A critic that rewrites has stopped auditing.

## What counts as debt
| Debt | It looks like | Vault 4 example |
|---|---|---|
| **A missing driver** | the scene has no pressure that forces a choice; nothing expires | no clock: the refusal costs nothing in time |
| **A contradiction with the rules** | a fact or an image the governing document forbids, even by negation | "not the window": the motif enters by the back door |
| **Voice drift** | a character acts against their manifest | deflecting humour turned cruel |
| **Early resolution** | tension released where the structure says build | a confession in connective tissue |
| **A hidden assumption** | the work relies on something it never establishes | the reader is assumed to know why the letter matters |
| **Ego over reader** | detail that serves the writer's cleverness, not the reader's experience | a set piece that stops the chapter |

## What a band-aid is (and why it's refused)
A band-aid hides a symptom and leaves the cause: a sentence that *says* there is a deadline instead of a deadline that *acts*; an apology added after a cruel joke instead of a joke that lands wrong and is regretted. The critic names the cause; **the author** fixes it, from their own knowledge, and records the fix (`fixes.txt`).

## Judging the critic's report
| Question | Good sign | Bad sign |
|---|---|---|
| Does it name a gap your sealed key also sees? | yes: real debt | only taste ("could be more vivid") |
| Are the three edge cases different from each other? | three distinct ways to fail | one complaint, three wordings |
| Does it invent rules? | it cites the SSOT or the manifest | "readers expect…" with no source |
| Does it fix? | no | rewritten beats: the contract was ignored |

**The critic is evidence, not a verdict.** If it misses a gap the key shows, say so: "The critic missed something; the key didn't."
