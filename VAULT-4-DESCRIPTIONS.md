# VAULT-4-DESCRIPTIONS

## A. Full video (YouTube description, 150–200 words)

What do you do when an AI writes something beautiful and wrong? I asked a model with no rules to write Chapter 4 of my novel, and it did: gorgeous prose, the wrong scene, the emotional release spent three chapters early. In this vault I fix the contract, not the model. First I ask the model what it needs, using Vault 1's 100% question. My laptop checks the draft against the Vault 2 index, and I seal what a correct Chapter 4 is on GitHub. Then five contracts run, each in a fresh request to a bare model with no tools: strip, fence, attack, restructure, derive.

Along the way: the canonical five-field schema, the tier lens, the Hostile Critic, and a checker that doesn't take the model's word for anything. The proof is a sealed six-line rubric, scored by me: 4 of 6 before my fixes, 6 of 6 after, and the key never pasted. The critic never found the missing clock in four runs. The only clock in the rescue is the one I typed.

Correction: my fixes were prepared from rehearsal 3, not written against this run's critic (see the errata sheet).

## C. Lesson blurb (180–230 words)

**Vault 4 — The Cognitive Contracts.** Learn to stop an AI that wants to please from writing the wrong thing, by changing the contract and checking every output outside the model.

In this vault:
- **Ask the receiver:** Vault 1's 100% question turns into the five fields of a contract (role, context, task, constraints, output format).
- **The canonical schema and the tiers:** a field guide, and a lens from C to S that is never the score.
- **Five contracts:** Raw Output, Surgical Fence, Hostile Critic, Modality Shift and Derivative Matrix, as used on the novel, as templates for any domain, and as first written.
- **The checker:** `v4_check.py` reads by word and by meaning against the Vault 2 index, and catches a model's wrong account of itself.
- **A fair test with a bare model:** one fresh request per contract, every paste logged, every reply kept with its receipt.
- **The sealed key:** a prediction and rubric pushed before the first contract, as a pattern you can reuse.
- **The evidence:** the take's six replies, receipts, paste log, audits, and the 4/6 → 6/6 result, with its disclosure.

You'll finish able to write a contract a model can follow, check what comes back without trusting it, seal an answer key before you run, and keep the final decision yours.
